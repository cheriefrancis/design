Contributions

What I worked on

Babbling Secrets was built by a three-person team. My contributions spanned programming, UX/UI, sound, and narrative — covering the connective tissue of the game experience.

Programming

Connected all three levels into a cohesive game loop, managed level transitions, and implemented the inventory and hint collection system that provides the basis for solving puzzles.

UX & UI Design

All 2D assets designed and built in Illustrator: the start menu, pause menu/instruction screens, inventory UI, pop-ups, and hints for Levels 1 and 2. Kept the visual language consistent with the Tower of Babel's aged aesthetic.

Sound Design

Selected and integrated all in-game audio — ambient, puzzle feedback, and transitional cues to reinforce the unsettling, shifting atmosphere of a world where language has lost its meaning.

Narrative Design

Wrote the opening and closing narrative sequences, establishing the premise and landing the ending. The story frames language collapse not as disaster but as invitation.

Intro & Outro Animations

Created the game's opening and closing animations in Illustrator — communicating the world and its stakes before a single button is pressed, and closing the loop at the end.

Level 1 exterior and top level Puzzle Design

Designed the exterior puzzle for Level 1, including the logic, hint objects, and player feedback to introduce the game's core interaction grammar without explicit instruction. Also did the combining fragment puzzle at the end to bring the levels together.

Narrative Design

A world where meaning shifts

The game is set in a modern Tower of Babel. Players ascend three levels, each containing an outer hallway puzzle and an inner puzzle room. Language and symbols act as remnants of meaning - their interpretation shifts with context.

The premise

In ancient Babylonia, everyone spoke one language. They tried to build a tower to the heavens and God stopped them by fracturing language itself.

The game doesn't explain this. It gives you fragments. Symbols that glow, objects that respond to touch, puzzles that seem to contradict their own rules. The player's job isn't to follow instructions — it's to learn how this broken world works, floor by floor, until the final level where all the fragments collected across the tower click into place.

Game Design

Rules that refuse to stay still

Interaction System

Players discover the world through proximity — objects glow, icons appear, outlines respond. The language of the interface is itself part of what's unstable.

  • Hint objects glow on approach
  • Interaction icons surface contextually
  • E key triggers hint interaction
  • AD for movement and WS for stair navigation
  • Left click to select, hold to drag

The Fragment System

Each puzzle room rewards completion with a fragment. These carry forward and are hidden until the combining scene at the top of the tower, where their assembled meaning is finally revealed.

  • One fragment per level
  • Collected into the player's hidden, secondary inventory
  • Assembled at the top level
  • Narrative resolution triggered on completion
