The brief

Build something for the online public

The prompt was open: create an experience under the theme of cyberpunk, designed for a public online audience. Rather than a generic neon-and-chrome aesthetic, the project became a speculative design exercise.

What would medical body modification look like in a biopunk future, and how would the interface differ between legal and underground providers?

The concept

Information as design language

The core insight driving the design: legitimate and black-market services would present the same product radically differently. The legal NBSI interface gives you everything — procedure details, risk profiles, 3D body models, clinical language, reassuring UI. The illegal side gives you almost nothing — a price, a name, a booking form. That asymmetry of information is the experience.

// NBSI — LICENSED

Transparent by design

Full procedure information, 3D anatomical previews, risk disclosure, clinical framing — the interface works to normalise what is, in reality, extreme body modification

// BLACK MARKET

Deliberately opaque

Price. Procedure name. A booking button. No explanation, no reassurance, no clinical language — the absence of information is intentional and unsettling

Research

Grounding speculation in plausibility

To make the modifications feel genuinely plausible rather than fantastical, research focused on real animal capabilities — what biological advantages do different species have, and could they be meaningfully applied to a human body? The result was a cross between eagle and octopus: visual acuity and tentacular manipulation.

Modifications were also tested informally for the "creepy threshold" — the legal side presents them clinically enough to feel almost acceptable, while the illegal side's patchier, less seamless execution deliberately crosses into discomfort.

Design process

How it came together

01

Research & concept definition

Animal capability research, speculative mod design, establishing the legal vs illegal contrast as the core design concept

02

3D asset creation in Blender

Eye, arm (without hand), and tentacle modifications modelled from scratch. Hand and head were imported models. Legal versions are seamless and clean; illegal versions are intentionally patchy and unsettling

03

Custom icons & UI frames in Illustrator

Interface frames, iconography, and decorative elements designed to feel like a functioning medical system — clinical enough to be believable

04

Figma prototyping

Full UI design for both sides of the experience, maintaining distinct visual languages — cyan/navy for legal, red/dark for illegal

05

Coded in HTML, CSS & JavaScript

Built as a fixed-size web experience — intentionally not responsive, reflecting the clinical, system-terminal aesthetic of a purpose-built medical interface
